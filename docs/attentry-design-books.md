# Attentry Design: books and references

Every book mentioned so far for Attentry Design, trimmed for overlap (2026-11-19). Local only, like
the other design docs. ★ = core: the most rules that can be measured from a video's source or its
rendered frames. Each area keeps the fewest books that cover it; the overlaps are listed at the end.

## Core (8)

1. ★ Bruce Block, *The Visual Story* (3rd ed.): contrast and affinity, the intensity curve
2. ★ Walter Murch, *In the Blink of an Eye* (2nd ed.): the Rule of Six, rhythm, eye-trace
3. ★ Frank Thomas & Ollie Johnston, *The Illusion of Life: Disney Animation*: easing, timing, staging
4. ★ Gustavo Mercado, *The Filmmaker's Eye*: composition per shot (thirds, headroom, lead room)
5. ★ Alexis Van Hurkman, *Color Correction Handbook*: scopes, skin tones, legal levels
6. ★ Robin Williams, *The Non-Designer's Design Book*: contrast, repetition, alignment, proximity
7. ★ Robert Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*: size, line length, spacing
8. ★ Jeff Bellantoni & Matt Woolman, *Type in Motion*: text that moves

## Dropped as redundant

- Ken Dancyger, *The Technique of Film and Video Editing*: a textbook covering Murch and Pearlman.
- Joseph Mascelli, *The Five C's of Cinematography*; Christopher Kenworthy, *Master Shots*;
  Jennifer van Sijll, *Cinematic Storytelling*: covered by Mercado and Katz.
- Jon Krasner, *Motion Graphic Design*: mostly history; Shaw covers the practice.
- Johannes Itten, *The Elements of Color*: color theory Albers covers. James Gurney, *Color and
  Light*: for painters. Charles Poynton, *Digital Video and HD*: the free ITU-R BT.709 covers what
  a rule needs.
- Ellen Lupton & Jennifer Cole Phillips, *Graphic Design: The New Basics*: covered by Williams.
  Rudolf Arnheim, *Art and Visual Perception*: academic, little to measure.
- Ellen Lupton, *Thinking with Type*; Erik Spiekermann, *Stop Stealing Sheep*: covered by
  Bringhurst and Butterick. Barbara Brownie, *Transforming Type*: covered by *Type in Motion*.
- Ron Brinkmann, *The Art and Science of Digital Compositing*; Steve Wright, *Digital Compositing
  for Film and Video*; *The VES Handbook of Visual Effects*: one VFX book is enough while Remotion
  videos rarely composite.
- Michel Chion, *Audio-Vision*: theory; Sonnenschein covers what a rule needs.

## Direction: the director's eye (added 2026-11-19)

Read 2026-11-23: Gilland *Elemental Magic* vol. 2 (vol. 1 not read yet), Yot, Gurney,
Bousquet, Mateu-Mestre and Glebas. Notes in
attentry-design-book-notes.md, "Direction books". Still unread from this list: Gilland vol. 1,
Bacher, MacLean, Azzi, Brown, the Pixar color scripts.

For the direction cards: what elements cause (light, water, fire, smoke, wind, motion) and how
directors and effects animators show it. ★ = get first.

| Book | What it gives the cards |
|---|---|
| ★ Joseph Gilland, *Elemental Magic* (vol. 1) | Fire, water, smoke, wind, splashes, as effects animators draw them: shapes, timing, how each element moves and breaks up |
| ★ Joseph Gilland, *Elemental Magic* (vol. 2) | More effects: explosions, magic, debris, stylized effects, and design of effects for a scene |
| ★ Richard Yot, *Light for Visual Artists* | How light behaves: falloff, bounce light, shadows, reflections, color of light, night and firelight |
| ★ James Gurney, *Color and Light* | Light and color in scenes: firelight, candlelight, sunsets, atmospheric depth, reflected light, water |
| ★ Michele Bousquet, *Physics for Animators* | Cause and effect in motion: gravity, momentum, drag, collisions, splashes, flying |
| ★ Marcos Mateu-Mestre, *Framed Ink* | Composition and lighting for visual storytelling: where to put the light, mood, leading the eye |
| Hans Bacher, *Dream Worlds* | Production design for animation: mood, light and color across a film |
| Francis Glebas, *Directing the Story* | Directing and staging for animation: shots, clarity, story beats |
| Fraser MacLean, *Setting the Scene* | Layout and staging in animation: depth, parallax, camera |
| Michael Azzi, *Pixel Logic* | Pixel art craft: palettes, dithering, light and shading, animation in pixels |
| Blain Brown, *Motion Picture and Video Lighting* | Practical film lighting: key, fill, practical lights, night scenes |
| *The Art of Pixar: The Complete Color Scripts* | Color scripts: how light and color carry a story beat by beat |

## Copy that reads like AI (added 2026-11-24)

For attentry-design-copy.md: the habits the research found (puffery, participle tails,
nominalizations, triads, uniform rhythm, no specifics) are exactly what good writing books teach
against. ★ = get first.

Read 2026-11-24 (two passes, notes in attentry-design-book-notes.md): Williams, Klinkenborg,
Heath, Handley and Zinsser from this list, plus Strunk, Lamott, Hopkins, Cialdini, Krug and
Fitzpatrick. Still missing: Ogilvy (★), Pinker, Sullivan, Clark.

| Book | What it gives |
|---|---|
| ★ Joseph M. Williams, *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace* | Nominalizations and actions as verbs: the exact grammar Reinhart et al. found LLMs overuse; turnable into rules (read 2026-11-24) |
| ★ Verlyn Klinkenborg, *Several Short Sentences About Writing* | Sentence-by-sentence craft and rhythm: the cure for uniform, frictionless sentences (read 2026-11-24) |
| ★ Chip & Dan Heath, *Made to Stick* | Concrete, specific, story-led messages: why "no specifics" kills copy (read 2026-11-24) |
| ★ Ann Handley, *Everybody Writes* | Marketing writing: voice, empathy for the reader, editing checklists (read 2026-11-24) |
| ★ David Ogilvy, *Ogilvy on Advertising* | Headlines and body copy from research: facts sell, puffery doesn't |
| Steven Pinker, *The Sense of Style* | Classic style, the curse of knowledge, why clichés and metadiscourse read as empty |
| William Zinsser, *On Writing Well* | Clutter, simplicity, plain verbs; the standard on cutting (read 2026-11-24) |
| Luke Sullivan, *Hey Whipple, Squeeze This* | Advertising craft: avoiding category language, finding the one true thing |
| Roy Peter Clark, *Writing Tools* | 50 concrete techniques (sentence length, specific detail, parallelism used on purpose) |

## Illustration that looks like AI (added 2026-11-24)

For attentry-design-illustration.md. The direction books (Gurney, Yot, Mateu-Mestre) already
cover light and staging; these add picture design, shape and the illustrator's choices.

Read 2026-11-24 (two passes): Bang, Gurney, Loomis, Payne. Still missing: Male, Leborg,
Stanchfield.

| Book | What it gives |
|---|---|
| ★ Molly Bang, *Picture This: How Pictures Work* | How shape, position, size and color make a picture feel; simple rules a check can use (centered and flat vs. diagonal and tense) (read 2026-11-24) |
| ★ James Gurney, *Imaginative Realism* | Designing scenes that don't exist so they read as real: the case AI art fails most (read 2026-11-24) |
| ★ Andrew Loomis, *Creative Illustration* | Classic illustration composition, value patterns, telling a story in one image (read 2026-11-24) |
| ★ Edgar Payne, *Composition of Outdoor Painting* | Composition patterns beyond the rule of thirds: the alternatives to AI's default layout (read 2026-11-24) |
| Alan Male, *Illustration: A Theoretical and Contextual Perspective* | What illustration is for (inform, persuade, narrate): judging an image by its job |
| Christian Leborg, *Visual Grammar* | The basic visual elements and relations, compact: vocabulary for rules |
| Walt Stanchfield, *Drawn to Life* | Gesture and intent in drawing: why stiff, posed figures read as fake |
| Hans Bacher, *Dream Worlds* (already listed) | Production design and visual development for animation |

## The five world styles (added 2026-11-24)

The styles the brief offers: hand-drawn, flat vector, isometric, 3D look, pixel art (chosen from
2026 trend reports; none measures performance, so these are well-supported picks, not a ranking).
Read 2026-11-24 (two passes, notes in attentry-design-book-notes.md):

| Book | For |
|---|---|
| Michael Azzi, *Pixel Logic* | Pixel art: lines, anti-aliasing, color, readability, mixels, isometric (read) |
| Jennifer Dawe & Matthew Humphries, *Make Your Own Pixel Art* | Pixel art basics (read) |
| Dan Roam, *The Back of the Napkin* | Hand-drawn explaining: the right picture for each kind of idea (read) |
| Von Glitschka, *Vector Basic Training* | Flat vector: clean anchor points and curves (read) |

Still to find: a book on isometric illustration, one on the 3D look, and Stanchfield's *Drawn to
Life* (gesture, for hand-drawn figures).

## Free standards and papers (read online)

- Netflix Timed Text Style Guide (reading speed, line length, duration)
- BBC subtitle guidelines (fonts, sizes, outlines over video)
- WCAG 2.2 (text contrast, flashing)
- ITU-R BT.1702 (flashing and epilepsy safety)
- ITU-R BT.709 (color, legal range)
- EBU R128 / ITU-R BS.1770 (loudness)
- Material Design 3 motion (easing curves, durations)
- Platform safe-zone pages (Shorts, Reels, TikTok)
- Kevin Larson's readability papers
